---
title: 'Morning Routines'
date: 07/23/2026
---

Today, I woke up 25 minutes after my alarm went off, to the sound of my
partner's own blaring phone. This is normal. We run the same schedule, more or
less, but I never get up at my alarm, so hers is always the backup. I sleepily
went to the bathroom to take my ever growing list of medications -- getting
older is hard -- and brush my teeth. I got back to my dresser, pulled out what
would be standard summer workwear, a plain t shirt and some comfortable shorts,
but then remembered I'm meant to go to the gym this morning, and went digging
through the unfolded laundry to find my workout attire.

I woke my kiddo up, I went downstairs, I made his favorite (pancakes out of a
box from the freezer, kefir, and apple slices), and made coffee. Our coffee
routine is a ritual in the truest sense; I always grind exactly 40 grams of
coffee, I always pre-wet the chemex filter, I always wring the filter out as
hard as I can and gently place it on top of the glass carafe and slowly swirl
water in, 100 grams to start and then 200 grams at a time til I get to 600. I
pour the coffee out into two small mugs I made last year. This routine has
continued almost unbroken every morning for the past eight years we've lived
together. And I sat down at the kitchen table with a Clif Brownie bar (my
current breakfast of choice) and little steamy mug of coffee.

This morning, my partner came over and rubbed my shoulders for a few. She gave
me an extra squeeze and an extra hug and an extra kiss. She had to leave
because traffic was getting bad, but it's okay; this is most mornings. The
kiddo and I sat and I scrolled LinkedIn and HackerNews while he played with hot
wheels. We had a small argument about wearing socks to school, and then I
loaded him up in the car and drove him to his preschool. I dropped him off,
gave him two big hugs because he was reluctant to go in, and told him I'd see
him this afternoon. The teacher pulled him away as he was a bit teary-eyed,
saying "Daddy has to go to work today, you'll see him later!"

I drove to the gym, and I worked out. And though my bones feel creaky and awful
as they usually do, I PB'ed a couple exercises and got a high five from one of
the trainers. It felt good.

And then I drove home to an empty house. This is normal.

I didn't sit down at the computer to start my work. This is _not_ normal. It's
been ten years since I didn't have a job to go. I don't really know what to do
with my now copious free time, and it makes me jittery and anxious. I am not
spinning out, that was yesterday's task, but today I am numb and I am sitting in
a big empty space with no one around me. I have things to do, we have another
baby arriving very soon and I have a thousand little tasks to get ready for her
to be here, and I now have the not-insignificant task of figuring out how I'm
going to earn money once my severance is up.

I'm going to start writing more again I think. This makes me feel better, for
some un-identifiable reason; maybe it's the practice of extracting the thoughts
from one part of my brain so they can be examined by another, or maybe it's the
catharsis of angrily typing on a keyboard, imagining every keystroke as a curse
laid on the nebulous targets of my ire.

I will pick my kid up from school this afternoon, and I will cook dinner for him
and for my partner and for her family (it's Thursday night Family Dinner,
another long standing tradition), and I will laugh and I will probably cry and I
will get big bear hugs from my father in law. I will be okay, at least today.
The routine will start again tomorrow, but hopefully the anger will be a little
less overbearing and the dreadful quiet will fade.
