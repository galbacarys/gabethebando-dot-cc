*Spoiler warning for Outer Wilds and Hadestown: The Musical. PLEASE go watch/play both if you haven't.*


You wake up next to a campfire. You roast a marshmallow, then clamber your way up through a clearing in a forest to an
observatory adorably made of wood. You get launch codes to a spaceship, dutifully waiting for you on top of the launch
platform next to where you awoke. And then, as you make your way to your ship, a three-eyed statue stares directly at
you and your memories replay in your mind. Shrugging this weirdness off, you head to your spaceship.

Twenty-two minutes later, you hear ominous music echoing, filling the periphery, almost narrowing your vision as it
takes its hold on you. You don't know it yet, but the haunting A-minor synthesizer bell chime is the death knell of the
star that has been slowly reddening overhead. You happen to look up at just the exact moment when the angry glowing red
orb collapses inward, becoming a violent shimmering blue, before radiating outward. As it reaches you, the scream of the
stellar wind overwhelms everything and your vision goes white.

And then your memories replay in your mind.

They begin right at the moment of your demise, playing backwards and accelerating until they get back to waking up next
to that campfire.

And then you take a deep, gasping breath, and wake back up next to the campfire. Everything is the same, except that you
have just watched yourself die. You, the player, are the main character now. The nameless creature in this game, begins
its loop anew, and as you begin exploring again, steering its will-less body around like a puppet, you begin to learn
what is happening around you. You speak with other inhabitants of this tiny pocket solar system, realizing with mounting
horror that they _simply don't remember any of this happening_. You're in a time loop, and the game doesn't seem to
acknowledge it at all. This is _Outer Wilds_.

This is fundamentally different than other video games, but it's not clear why at first. Yes, of course, when you play a
game and you die, you "respawn" somewhere usually and keep on playing, but it's in the diegesis[^1] of the game that you
have "reset" back to that spot, continuing the gameplay as if you hadn't died. Outer Wilds is different: the memory you,
the person playing the game, carry from iteration to iteration 

Outer Wilds is the only game that has ever made me cry. It's the only game that has filled me with the existential void
that comes with realizing that you are a tiny speck -- _less than_ a tiny speck -- in this vast, beautiful, weird,
unfathomable universe.

[^1]: Diegesis is this sort of fuzzy concept that roughly means "in the logic of the story". A really famous example is
    the diegetic (adjective form of the word) menuing you do on the Pip-Boy in the Fallout games.
