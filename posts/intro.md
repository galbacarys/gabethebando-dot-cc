title: The inevitable first post
date: 03/27/2026
---

Something very specific drove me to tear down my whole site and start over.

I had a little blog I was occasionally updating on
[Mataroa](https://mataroa.blog), an _excellent_ little blogging platform that I
have absolutely no ill will for, and which deserves your attention and money if
you can afford it.

But recently, I've been using a lot of AI at work. And I do mean, a _lot_.
Claude Code has been effectively doing most of my job for me, better than I can
even do it, or at least that's what the gnawing anxiety inside me was saying.
Of course, that isn't true in the strictest sense; I am doing a _lot_ of
thinky-feely work and the fact that the coding part of my job has been
outsourced to an over-caffeinated intern isn't necessarily anathema to my still
having a job in 6 months, but there is something deeply demoralizing about
realizing that the thing you used to _love_ - that is, writing code, watching
it run, thinking through the deep intricate details of the work you are trying
to do, feeling good about how you solved a problem, all of that is just...gone.

I spent a few weeks trying to rationalize. Well of _course_ I am not feeling
that part of my job, it wasn't even fun anyway! Probably! Right? That gnawing
anxiety increased though, and I realized that in the last 6 months I had
written maybe 100 lines of code fully by myself, mostly to fix mistakes Claude
made, and every time I did it it was getting harder. I was losing the magic, so
to speak. And so, One Sunday evening a week or so ago, I spun up a new box on
DigitalOcean (the very box you're speaking to now!), and began coding again.

I'm going to write a little page about the infrastructure of this site; it's
not super advanced or anything, it's mostly just Flask and SQLite (and the
SQLite part doesn't even really do anything yet), but it's _mine_. I wrote all
the code. I wrote the shitty HTML. I wrote the shitty deployment scripts. I
wrote the almost passable Python. All the words were always my own, but now the
code is too. I'm hoping that this will give me some peace, at last, and that I
will feel like an artisan doing this job again.

I used to _love_ coding. I would do [Project Euler](https://projecteuler.net/)
puzzles just to prove I could. I learned programming languages just to know
them. I haven't felt that love in a long time. Maybe this will be a place where
I can re-engage that love and feel it as deeply as I once did.
