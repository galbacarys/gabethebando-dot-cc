_Baba is You_ remains one of my favorite puzzle games of all time. I would put it in the same
category as games like _Blue Prince_, _Animal Well_, and _Outer Wilds_ in terms of enjoyability.
I recently started playing it again and I wanted to take a bit of time and space to talk about
why this silly little game about a...sheep? Let's go with sheep...has captivated me for the better
part of seven years[^1] and what it has to teach about puzzle design.

## What is Baba is You?

What an atrocious sentence. Okay, _Baba is You_ is a 2019 Sokoban[^2]-like puzzle game by [Hempuli](https://hempuli.com/baba/),
a Finnish game designer whom I greatly admire (and you should go check out! They are putting out
new weird little games to this day). The twist, of course, is what makes _Baba_ so unique.

All around the screen are text blocks, which at first glance spell out simple rules: for example,
`[BABA] [IS] [YOU]`. From the very first puzzle of the game, you're required to push these text
blocks together. You quickly learn simple things like "YOU" indicates what character you can
control, and if you change e.g. `[BABA] [IS] [YOU]` to `[WALL] [IS] [YOU]`, well, you're
controlling every wall on screen rather than the fluffy lil sheep you start as. This is a neat
enough concept for a game, and I think the first 8 or 10 levels do a really good job of just 
getting you up to speed with the basics of what all these pieces mean.

[^1]: Oh fuck.
[^2]: Sokoban is a 1981 Japanese puzzle video game that involves pushing boxes around a grid to
      solve puzzles. You've played one of these before, even if you don't know the word.
